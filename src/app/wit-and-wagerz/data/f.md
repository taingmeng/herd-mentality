## 🚀 Section 1: Space Mining, Rockets & Deep Universe Astrophysics (1–40)

**1. How many months did it take NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft to reach interstellar space after its launch?**

* **Answer:** 420 months (Exactly 35 years)
* **Fun Fact:** Launched in 1977, it officially crossed the heliopause—the boundary where the sun's solar wind meets the interstellar medium—in August 2012.

**2. How many operational robotic rovers are currently active on the surface of Mars as of 2026?**

* **Answer:** 2
* **Fun Fact:** NASA's *Curiosity* (landed 2012) and *Perseverance* (landed 2021) continue to drive across the Martian terrain, while China's Zhurong is currently inactive.

**3. What is the escape velocity needed to completely break free of Earth's gravity, in miles per hour?**

* **Answer:** 25,020 mph
* **Fun Fact:** This translates to roughly 7 miles per second, meaning any spacecraft leaving Earth for the Moon or Mars must achieve this incredible speed.

**4. How many individual main engines ignite simultaneously on the first-stage booster of a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket?**

* **Answer:** 27
* **Fun Fact:** The rocket straps three Falcon 9 cores together, requiring 27 Merlin engines to synchronize perfectly at liftoff to unleash over 5 million pounds of thrust.

**5. In what year did astronomers discover 16 Psyche, the famous metallic asteroid estimated to hold quadrillions of dollars in raw iron and nickel?**

* **Answer:** 1852
* **Fun Fact:** Discovered by Italian astronomer Annibale de Gasparis, NASA launched a dedicated spacecraft to study this metal world, which arrived in orbit recently.

**6. How many planets in our solar system possess a visible, ring system circling their atmosphere?**

* **Answer:** 4
* **Fun Fact:** While Saturn's rings are the most famous and brilliant, Jupiter, Uranus, and Neptune also feature faint, dark rings made of dust and rock fragments.

**7. In what year did the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission successfully land a probe on the surface of a moving comet?**

* **Answer:** 2014
* **Fun Fact:** The *Philae* lander bounced twice on the surface of Comet 67P before settling in the shadow of a cliff, sending back data for days before its batteries died.

**8. How many thousands of light-years wide is our home galaxy, the Milky Way?**

* **Answer:** 100 thousand light-years
* **Fun Fact:** If you could travel at the speed of light (186,000 miles per second), it would still take you 100,000 years to fly from one edge of our galaxy to the other.

**9. In what year did the historic Apollo 13 mission experience an oxygen tank explosion, forcing an emergency loop around the Moon?**

* **Answer:** 1970
* **Fun Fact:** The crew survived by utilizing the Lunar Module as a temporary lifeboat, managing cold temperatures and rising carbon dioxide levels to return safely.

**10. How many distinct moons are currently confirmed to be orbiting the gas giant Jupiter?**

* **Answer:** 95
* **Fun Fact:** Jupiter and Saturn are locked in a continuous "moon race" as advanced ground telescopes locate smaller, irregular satellites trapped in their gravitational nets.

**11. In what year was the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) successfully launched into space, clearing a path to view the early universe?**

* **Answer:** 2021
* **Fun Fact:** Orbiting a million miles away from Earth at the L2 Lagrange point, its tennis-court-sized sunshield keeps its infrared mirrors chilled near absolute zero.

**12. How many minutes long was the historic first American human spaceflight executed by Alan Shepard in 1961?**

* **Answer:** 15 minutes
* **Fun Fact:** Unlike Yuri Gagarin's full orbital flight, Shepard’s *Freedom 7* mission was a suborbital arc, reaching an altitude of 116 miles before splashing down in the Atlantic.

**13. What is the surface temperature of the planet Mercury on its sun-facing side, in degrees Fahrenheit?**

* **Answer:** 800°F
* **Fun Fact:** Because Mercury lacks an atmosphere to trap heat, its night side drops to a blistering -290°F, creating the most extreme temperature swing in the solar system.

**14. How many years does it take the dwarf planet Pluto to complete one single orbit around the Sun?**

* **Answer:** 248 years
* **Fun Fact:** Since its discovery in 1930, Pluto has not yet completed a single full orbit around the sun, and won't do so until the year 2178.

**15. In what year did NASA's New Horizons spacecraft execute its historic close flyby of Pluto, capturing high-definition images of its heart-shaped plain?**

* **Answer:** 2015
* **Fun Fact:** The spacecraft traveled for over 9 full years to reach Pluto, zooming past the dwarf planet at over 30,000 mph before continuing into the Kuiper Belt.

**16. How many miles wide is the largest known impact crater in our solar system (the Hellas Planitia basin on Mars)?**

* **Answer:** 1,400 miles
* **Fun Fact:** The massive crater was carved by a giant asteroid impact billions of years ago, dropping down nearly 5 miles deep—deep enough to hold Mount Everest.

**17. In what year did the Pioneer 10 spacecraft become the first man-made object to fly through the solar system's asteroid belt safely?**

* **Fun Fact:** Before Pioneer 10, scientists feared the asteroid belt was a dense wall of rocks that would instantly shred any spacecraft trying to cross it.
